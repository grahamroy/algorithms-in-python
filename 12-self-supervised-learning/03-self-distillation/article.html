Part 1 ended on a measured cliffhanger. Keep the pull — make two augmented views of the same image agree — and delete the crowd of negatives pushing back, and the embedding collapses 158-fold: every image mapped to the same point, loss perfectly minimised, representation worthless. The obvious conclusion was that the crowd is what holds the space open.

DINO (Caron et al., 2021 — self-DIstillation with NO labels) pulls with no negatives anyway, and does not fall. Two networks, same architecture. The student is trained; the teacher is not trained at all — it is an exponential moving average of the student’s own weights. The student’s own past, distilled, made into the target it chases:

    teacher sees view A  ->  soft targets (sharpened, centred)
    student sees view B  ->  cross-entropy toward the targets
    (and symmetrically, B -> A;  teacher <- EMA(student))

Both networks end in a small head over K “prototypes” — learned soft categories, no labels anywhere. What keeps the tightrope walkable is a pair of opposing tweaks to the teacher’s softmax. Centring subtracts a running mean of teacher logits, killing the everything-onto-one-prototype solution. Sharpening gives the teacher a lower softmax temperature than the student, killing the everything-uniform solution. A running mean and a temperature — that is the entire safety apparatus.

The stage is shape world, and the companion script retrains Part 1’s contrastive encoder and Part 1’s pull-only collapse live, so all three sit on one measured table. The student’s backward pass — through both softmaxes, teacher and centre held fixed — is hand-derived and audited at startup (8.83e-06 against central differences).

A worked example: the walk, the tightrope, the pace

Agreement without the crowd

      500        83.3%           90.1%
     1000        98.7%           99.0%
     3000        99.9%           99.9%

Read the bottom table’s rows in order. The pull-only row is Part 1’s collapse, reproduced live: probes at chance, embedding spread three orders of magnitude below healthy. DINO is the same pull — two views, agree, no negatives anywhere in the loss — and it matches the full NT-Xent contrastive encoder on every column: 94.5% on fifteen labels against 95.1%, 99.9% with all labels against 99.7%, spread fully held open. The crowd of negatives, whose absence looked fatal in Part 1, turned out to be optional. What is not optional is the next demo.

The tightrope: centring × sharpening

Two failure modes wait, one on each side. H(per) is the mean per-sample entropy of the teacher’s predictions; H(mean) is the entropy of the average prediction; log K = 3.47:

    recipe             probe-all   H(per)   H(mean)   verdict
    centre + sharpen     99.9%      0.14     2.09    healthy
    centre only          54.7%      3.47     3.47    uniform collapse
    sharpen only         69.8%      0.00     0.51    one-hot collapse
    neither              52.0%      3.15     3.15    uniform collapse

Centring alone: per-sample entropy pinned at exactly log K — the teacher says “anything is fine” and the student agrees to say nothing; the safest possible agreement is total vagueness. Sharpening alone: entropy 0.00 with the batch’s mass crowded onto one prototype — perfectly confident, perfectly empty; the safest possible agreement is one answer for everything. The two tweaks fail in opposite directions, which is exactly why the pair works: together they pin the only useful operating point — confident about each image (0.14 nats), diverse across images (2.09 nats). This is the DINO paper’s central ablation, reproduced here down to the entropies, and it is the honest answer to “where did the negatives’ job go?”: into a running mean and a temperature.

The teacher’s pace

The teacher is the student’s own past, averaged with momentum m. How much past is the right amount?

    momentum    probe-all    spread
     0.000       67.3%     0.1107
     0.900       99.9%     0.1520
     0.990       99.9%     0.1524
     0.999       99.1%     0.1595

Momentum zero — the teacher is the student — and the chase degenerates: a target that moves with every step teaches little. Momentum 0.9 to 0.99 works; 0.999 remembers too much for a 3,000-step budget. And one piece of folklore, checked honestly: the DINO paper reports that the EMA teacher outperforms its student during training. Here the teacher trails early — 54.7% against the student’s 59.2% at step 100 — and converges to a tie. At this scale the teacher’s job is not to be better; it is to be stable: slow enough to be chased, which is precisely what the momentum-zero row loses. That is the deepest reading of the whole negative-free family — BYOL’s predictor head, SimSiam’s stop-gradient, DINO’s EMA teacher differ in decoration, but each one is an asymmetry that keeps the target out of lockstep with the learner. Break the asymmetry and you are back at Part 1’s single point.

Big-O and complexity

![[BIG-O TABLE IMAGE]]

DINO’s bill is Part 2’s, not Part 1’s: two encoder passes per view but no O(B²) similarity matrix — the quadratic crowd line is simply gone, which is why negative-free methods scale to batch sizes and devices the contrastive recipe struggled with. The teacher costs one extra forward pass and one EMA update (O(P) — cheaper than a gradient step, since nothing is backpropagated through it), and centring is a running mean, O(B · K). The head’s prototypes add a d × K matrix. As with Part 1, adaptation downstream is a linear probe over frozen features — and at real scale it is DINO’s descendants, not the contrastive originals, whose frozen features became the default: the probe column of DEMO 1 at four thousand times the size.

Where the shadows went

DINO’s headline at scale was not the probe numbers — it was what appeared unasked: the attention maps of a DINO-trained vision transformer segment objects from their backgrounds with no supervision whatsoever, sharp enough to use as masks. The recipe’s descendants took over the field. DINOv2 added Part 2’s masked prediction to the self-distillation loss — this section’s two philosophies, agreement and reconstruction, in one objective — and its frozen features now sit under depth-estimation, segmentation and retrieval systems as the closest thing vision has to a universal backbone. BYOL and SimSiam mapped the rest of the negative-free territory (a predictor head and a stop-gradient are asymmetries enough); EMA teachers now stabilise semi-supervised learning, masked distillation (data2vec across speech, vision and text), and reinforcement-learning targets — Section 8’s DQN target network was this same idea wearing older clothes. Wherever a model must learn from its own outputs without spiralling, a slower copy of itself is the standing answer.

What comes next

Part 4, Momentum Contrast (MoCo), closes the section by going back to Part 1’s crowd and asking the economic question: if negatives are the signal, must they all fit in one batch? MoCo’s queue of past embeddings — served by the same EMA encoder this article just built — bought SimCLR-sized crowds at a fraction of the memory, and completes the section’s family picture: every method is a different answer to where the second view’s yardstick comes from.

The complete code

The full script is on GitHub — grab it and run it:

dino.py

Run it with:

pip install numpy

It needs only numpy and runs in about seven minutes. Everything is from scratch: student, EMA teacher, prototype head, centring and sharpening, the symmetric distillation loss with hand-derived audited gradients, live retrains of Part 1’s contrastive and pull-only baselines, and the entropy instruments for the collapse taxonomy. The headline insight worth pinning to the wall: the crowd of negatives was never the point — a slow-moving copy of yourself is yardstick enough: DINO matches the contrastive encoder (94.5%/99.9% vs 95.1%/99.7%) with zero negatives while the naive version of the same pull collapses to chance at spread 0.0011; the tightrope is held by a running mean and a temperature, whose solo failures are opposite and exact (centring alone: entropy pinned at log K, uniform collapse; sharpening alone: entropy 0.00, one-hot collapse; together: 0.14 within, 2.09 across); and the teacher earns its keep not by being better — it trails its student early, 54.7% to 59.2% — but by being stable, the asymmetry every working negative-free method shares.
