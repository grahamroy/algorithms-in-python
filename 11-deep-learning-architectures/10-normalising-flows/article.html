The section’s generative quartet closes with the purist. The GAN (Part 7) never wrote a loss down. The VAE (Part 8) wrote one, but only a bound. Diffusion (Part 9) regressed its way around the problem. The normalising flow (RealNVP: Dinh et al., 2017) refuses every one of those compromises: build the map from noise to data out of invertible steps only, and the model computes the exact probability of any point you show it — to the digit:

log p(x)  =  log N( f(x); 0, I )  +  log |det df/dx|

Read it as bookkeeping. Push the data point x through an invertible map f to noise-space; score it under a standard Gaussian; then add the log-determinant of the Jacobian — the exact accounting of how much f stretched or squeezed space on the way. Every unit of probability is conserved; nothing is approximated; the training loss is the log-likelihood.

The catch is the construction: every layer must be invertible, and its Jacobian determinant affordable. The affine coupling layer is the trick that made this practical. Split the coordinates; one half passes through untouched and sets the scale and shift for the other:

y_b = x_b * exp(s(x_a)) + t(x_a),      log|det| = s(x_a)

Inverting it is one subtraction and one division — the passthrough half is unchanged, so s and t can be recomputed exactly. The log-determinant is read off the wire. s and t come from an ordinary little MLP that never needs inverting, because the wiring, not the network, carries the invertibility. Stack the couplings, alternating which coordinate leads, and noise warps into data. Sampling is one inverse pass; encoding is one forward pass; both are exact.

The companion script hand-derives the backward pass through the couplings and the log-det terms, audited at startup (1.86e-08 against central differences), and the stage is Parts 7–9’s — the two spirals, the ring, the shared yardsticks — with the GAN, VAE and diffusion model retrained live for the quartet’s final table.

A worked example: the number, the tax, the quartet

The exact number

     step   eval NLL   q@0.10   q@0.05   coverage
     2000     1.247     95.7%    53.4%     98.3%
     4000     1.297     90.1%    50.5%     98.3%
     6000     1.217     94.3%    53.7%    100.0%
     8000     1.208     96.7%    54.9%     98.3%

The loss is exact — and the eval NLL still wobbles (1.297 at step 4000 after 1.247), because exactness of the objective does not make the optimisation monotone. The number is honest; the descent is still SGD. Then the receipts, which no other architecture in this section can print:

    integral of p(x) over the plane : 1.0000
    round trip max |x - f_inv(f(x))|: 3.94e-15
    z of real data: mean [0.032 0.016], var [0.864 1.221]

Three different kinds of exactness. The model’s density, numerically integrated over the plane, comes back 1.0000 — a genuine probability distribution, verified by quadrature, not by faith. The round trip x → z → x reproduces the data to 3.94e-15 — floating-point roundoff. Part 8’s VAE reconstructed with error 0.002 and called it good; the flow is the autoencoder that cannot lie, because reconstruction is algebra, not learning. And real data pushed to noise-space lands almost exactly on the standard Gaussian the model promised.

Then it prices individual points:

    log p ( 0.12, 0.00)  on an arm        :      -1.935
    log p ( 0.00, 0.00)  between the arms :      -1.988
    log p ( 2.00, 2.00)  far off-manifold : -147701.380

A point two units off the manifold is charged into oblivion — log-probability minus one hundred and forty-seven thousand — exactly as it should be. But look at the middle row. The empty spot between the arms is priced at nearly the same as a spot on an arm. That is not a bug in training. Hold the thought.

The topology tax

A flow is a continuous bijection: it can bend the plane, stretch it, fold it — but never tear it. Depth buys precision:

    couplings   eval NLL   q@0.10   off-manifold
        2        1.326     89.6%       7.3%
        4        1.211     95.0%       1.8%
        8        1.208     96.7%       1.1%
       16        1.224     94.7%       2.5%

Two couplings are crude, eight polish stray mass down to 1.1%, sixteen buy nothing further. But no depth can do what the ring of eight Gaussians demands: cut one connected blob of noise into eight islands. Twelve couplings, three seeds:

    seed 0: modes 8/8   on-mode mass 45%
    seed 1: modes 8/8   on-mode mass 33%
    seed 2: modes 8/8   on-mode mass 58%

Every mode, every seed — a flow cannot drop an island, because dropping one would tear the map; mode collapse is topologically forbidden. But 42–67% of the mass lands off-mode, in filaments of probability connecting islands the data never connected. And because the flow prices points exactly, the tax can be itemised:

    p at a mode centre  :  1.22e+00
    p BETWEEN two modes :  1.11e+00
    p at the ring centre:  5.56e-06
    TRUE p between modes:  9.51e-13

The model prices the empty midpoint between two modes at 1.11 — 91% of a mode centre’s 1.22 — where the true density is 9.5e-13. Twelve orders of magnitude of topology tax, printed to the digit by the only architecture capable of printing it. This is the article’s punchline pair: the flow’s superpower and its signature failure are the same fact. The map must be smooth, invertible and whole — so it can tell you the exact price of anything, including the exact price of its own inability to tear.

The quartet

Four articles, four generative architectures, one stage — GAN, VAE and diffusion retrained live with their own seeds and budgets, diffusion at its best 20-pass setting:

                          passes   q@0.10   q@0.05   coverage
    GAN (Part 7)             1     98.7%    58.0%     81.7%
    VAE (Part 8)             1     89.5%    51.3%     98.3%
    diffusion (Part 9)      20     98.7%    55.6%    100.0%
    flow (this article)      1     96.7%    54.9%     98.3%
    real data                -     99.5%    77.3%     93.3%
    pure noise (floor)       -     40.7%    22.9%     95.0%

The section’s generative story in six rows. The GAN: sharpest, cheapest, 18% of the arc missing — the mode-seeking game. The VAE: everything covered, softly — the honest bound. Diffusion: sharp and covering at twenty passes — the trade refused, paid in compute. The flow: one pass, honest middle scores on every yardstick — and the only row that can tell you, exactly, how probable any of its opinions are. Four answers to the same question: where should the difficulty live? In the opponent, in the encoder, in the schedule — or in the Jacobian.

Big-O and complexity

![[BIG-O TABLE IMAGE]]

Everything a flow does costs one pass: sampling is one inverse sweep through the couplings, O(K · h²); exact density evaluation — the thing nobody else sells at any price — is one forward sweep of the same cost; training needs no sampling loop, no second network, no schedule. The structural bill is paid elsewhere: coupling layers move only half the coordinates per step, so flows need depth K where a free architecture needs less, and every hidden representation must stay the same dimension as the data — no bottlenecks, no compression, which at image scale makes flows memory-hungry (Glow’s multi-scale factoring exists precisely to fight this). The general lesson of the table: constraints move cost around, never remove it. The GAN paid in stability, the VAE in blur, diffusion in passes; the flow pays in architecture.

Where the exact numbers went

Flows never took the image-generation crown — RealNVP and Glow made striking faces in 2017–18, but the topology tax and the equal-width constraint kept them behind GANs, then diffusion. They won everywhere the number matters more than the picture. Density estimation and anomaly detection: score a point, flag the cheap ones — the receipt DEMO 1 printed is the product. Simulation-based inference in physics and astronomy, where posteriors must be exact and reusable. Variational posteriors inside other models — a flow can replace the VAE’s diagonal Gaussian with something honest about correlations. Molecular Boltzmann generators, where free-energy differences demand real log-likelihoods. And the idea’s descendants run the field: continuous flows became neural ODEs; flow matching — training a continuous flow with a diffusion-style regression instead of a Jacobian — now trains some of the strongest image and video models, a literal merger of this article’s mechanism with Part 9’s loss. The purist’s constraint relaxed, the purist’s exact change kept.
