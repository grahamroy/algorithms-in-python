# Diffusion Models — A Thousand Tiny Problems Instead of One Impossible One

### *Algorithms in Python --- Deep Learning Architectures, Part 9*

---

Part 7 generated by playing a game, and paid in instability.
Part 8 wrote an honest loss, and paid in blur. The **diffusion
model** (Sohl-Dickstein et al., 2015; Ho et al., *DDPM*, 2020)
makes the strangest bargain of the three — it deletes the hard
half of the machine.

In a VAE, the encoder had to be *learned*: a network trained to
find codes the decoder could use. Diffusion fixes the encoder
to something almost insultingly simple — **add noise**. Not
once: in `T` graded doses, until nothing of the data remains:

```
x_t  =  sqrt(abar_t) * x0  +  sqrt(1 - abar_t) * eps
```

where `abar_t` slides from 1 (clean) to nearly 0 (pure static)
along a fixed schedule. No parameters, nothing to train, and
every intermediate `x_t` comes with a free label — the exact
noise `eps` that was mixed in.

That free label is the whole design. The *only* network in a
diffusion model is a denoiser, `eps_theta(x_t, t)`, trained to
look at a noisy point and predict the noise inside it. The loss
is the simplest in this entire section — a plain regression,
`||eps - eps_theta(x_t, t)||²` — no adversary, no balance of
speeds, no KL bookkeeping; and it is secretly Part 8's ELBO in
disguise (the DDPM paper derives it as exactly that, with the
encoder's terms constant because the encoder has no
parameters). Generation runs the staircase backwards: start
from pure noise, and take `T` model passes, each subtracting
the slice of noise its schedule step added.

One impossible problem — "turn static into data" — has been
replaced by a thousand tiny ones: "remove a little noise from
something almost clean". Each tiny problem is easy, each is
graded by regression, and the companion script's hand-derived
backward pass is audited at startup (2.58e-10 against central
differences).

## A worked example: the triptych, the bill, the ring

### The fixed encoder, and the triptych

Training the denoiser on the spirals, sampling with all 100
passes at each checkpoint:

```
     step    q@0.10   q@0.05   coverage
     4000     90.1%    49.9%    100.0%
     8000     92.0%    52.6%    100.0%
    12000     93.5%    52.9%    100.0%
    16000     94.5%    53.0%    100.0%
```

Coverage is total from the first checkpoint — like the VAE,
diffusion is mode-covering by construction, because the
regression is graded on real points drawn from *everywhere* in
the data. Then the section's reunion table:

```
                              q@0.10   q@0.05   coverage
    GAN (Part 7), 1 pass       98.7%    58.0%     81.7%
    VAE (Part 8), 1 pass       89.5%    51.3%     98.3%
    diffusion, 100 passes      94.5%    53.0%    100.0%
    diffusion,  20 passes      98.7%    55.6%    100.0%
    real data                  99.5%    77.3%     93.3%
    pure noise (floor)         40.7%    22.9%     95.0%
```

Read the fourth row against the first two. The 20-pass
diffusion model matches the GAN's quality at 0.10 **exactly** —
98.7% against 98.7% — while covering 100.0% of the arc the GAN
leaves 18% short. Sharp *and* covering: the trade Parts 7 and 8
measured against each other, refused. Nothing is free — the
invoice simply moved. Twenty forward passes per sample, against
their one.

(The floor row is worth a glance: a cloud of pure noise scores
40.7% on the loose yardstick. Every anchor keeps the numbers
above it honest.)

### The bill of passes, two-sided

The same trained network, sampled on strided subsets of its
100-step schedule:

```
    passes    q@0.10   q@0.05   coverage
      100      94.5%    53.0%    100.0%
       50      96.1%    55.2%    100.0%
       20      98.7%    55.6%    100.0%
       10      99.0%    54.3%     90.0%
        5      94.9%    53.8%     55.0%
        2       0.2%     0.2%      0.0%
        1       0.2%     0.2%      0.0%
    (floor)    40.7%    22.9%     95.0%
```

Both edges of this table cut. Downward from 100, quality
*rises* — to a peak around 10–20 passes. That is not a
paradox: every stochastic sampling step is a chance for an
imperfect network to inject its own error, so fewer, bigger,
cleaner steps win. Measured here at toy scale; discovered at
real scale as **DDIM** and the sampler race that followed —
the finding that turned thousand-step models into
twenty-step products.

Then the cliff. Five passes lose almost half the coverage. Two
passes score 0.2% — *worse than pure noise*, because the model
collapses everything toward a point the yardstick rejects. One
pass returns the dataset's mean. The staircase can be strided,
not skipped — and the reason is visible in the trajectory,
the median distance from the samples to the true manifold
after each stage of the walk:

```
     after step   median distance
       t =  99       0.259
       t =  75       0.262
       t =  50       0.258
       t =  25       0.085
       t =  10       0.057
       t =   0       0.047
```

Nothing happens for half the walk — 0.259 to 0.258 — then the
shape snaps in between t=50 and t=25, and the last ten steps
polish detail down to 0.047, the data's own noise floor.
Coarse structure first, fine detail last: a frequency anneal.
Early steps decide *where mass goes*; late steps decide *what
it looks like up close*. That is why the process must be
gradual, and it is the honest content of the phrase "diffusion
models are hard to skip".

### The ring: what compute buys

Parts 7 and 8 measured the ring as a trade — the healthy GAN
put 81% of its mass on-mode but held only 3/8 modes when its
race went wrong; the VAE kept 8/8 on every seed at 39–59%
on-mode. Diffusion at the same budget (hidden 128, 10k steps),
three seeds:

```
    seed 0: modes 8/8   on-mode mass 26%
    seed 1: modes 8/8   on-mode mass 28%
    seed 2: modes 8/8   on-mode mass 27%
```

All modes, every seed — and the blurriest result in the
section. At matched budget the staircase is honest but fuzzy.
Now triple it — hidden 256, batch 256, 30k steps:

```
    big model: modes 8/8   on-mode mass 74%
```

74% on-mode, approaching the GAN's 81% sharpness while holding
every mode the GAN dropped — and note what was *absent* on the
way: no game, no balance of speeds, no posterior to collapse.
The loss is a regression and it just descends. **Diffusion
converts compute into sharpness without surrendering
coverage** — paid in training FLOPs and sampling passes, that
conversion is the last five years of generative modelling in
one sentence.

## Big-O and complexity

![[BIG-O TABLE IMAGE]]

Training is cheap per step — one forward and backward pass of
a single network, `O(b · L · h²)`, with the noising encoder
costing a trivial `O(b · d)` because it has no parameters at
all. The bill arrives at sampling: `T` sequential model passes
per sample, `O(T · L · h²)` — the column Part 7's table
flagged in advance — and the passes are *inherently*
sequential; step `t` needs step `t+1`'s output, so parallelism
buys nothing within one sample. That single line finances the
entire samplers literature: striding (this article's DEMO 2),
DDIM, higher-order ODE solvers, distillation down to one step
— all of it is engineering against `T`. Evaluation inherits
the VAE's virtue in principle (the loss bounds a likelihood)
but in practice the field grades samples, and the training
loss is at least a loss — noisy but descending, with none of
the GAN's untrustworthy orbit.

## Where the staircases went

Diffusion took the crown Part 7's epilogue mentioned, and it
took it with this article's exact ingredients scaled up.
DDPM's spiral-drawing regression became image generation with
a U-Net for the denoiser; **latent diffusion** moved the
staircase inside a VAE's compressed space — Part 8's machinery
as the stage floor — and became Stable Diffusion; guidance
(classifier-free) turned the same denoiser into a
text-conditioned one, and DALL·E 2, Imagen and Midjourney
followed. The sampler
race (DEMO 2, industrialised) cut a thousand passes to twenty,
then distillation cut twenty toward one — trading back toward
GAN economics from a model that never trained like a GAN. Off
the image track the same staircase now writes audio, video,
molecular conformations and robot trajectories; anywhere a
distribution is too complicated to draw in one step, drawing
it in a thousand small ones has become the default. And the
family view worth keeping: GAN, VAE and diffusion are not
rivals so much as three answers to *where the difficulty
lives* — in the opponent, in the encoder, or in the schedule.

## The complete code

The full script is on GitHub — grab it and run it:

[**diffusion.py**](https://github.com/grahamroy/algorithms-in-python/blob/main/11-deep-learning-architectures/09-diffusion-models/diffusion.py)

Run it with:

```bash
pip install numpy
python diffusion.py
```

It needs only `numpy` and runs in about three minutes — the
big ring model is most of the bill, and that is rather the
point. Everything is from scratch: the noising schedule, the
sinusoidal time embedding, the eps-prediction network with its
hand-derived, audited backward pass, EMA weights, ancestral
and strided sampling, and live retrains of Part 7's GAN and
Part 8's VAE for the reunion table. The headline insight worth
pinning to the wall: **diffusion deletes the hard half of the
generative problem — its encoder is fixed noise, its loss a
plain regression secretly equal to the VAE's ELBO — and by
splitting one impossible problem into a hundred graded ones it
refuses the section's central trade: 20-pass sampling matches
the GAN's 98.7% quality at 100% coverage, no game and no
collapse anywhere in training; the price surfaces as passes
(quality peaks at 10–20 strided steps — the DDIM discovery —
then falls off a cliff to below-noise-floor at 2) and as
compute (the ring sharpens from 26% to 74% on-mode with 3x the
budget, without losing a mode) — sharpness bought with FLOPs,
not with stability**.
